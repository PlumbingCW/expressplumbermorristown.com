If a drain has backed up again after a cable machine, or a cable jammed halfway down the line, hydro jetting is usually the next step. Morristown Express Plumbing dispatches plumbers for hydro jetting in Morristown, NJ, with 24/7 emergency service when a line has stopped entirely. Until the crew arrives, keep water use to a minimum, keep any basement floor drain clear of stored items, and do not pour anything into the pipe.

Jetting pumps water at high pressure through a hose with a nozzle that fires jets forward and backward. The spray shreds soft buildup and strips the pipe wall rather than poking one channel through it. It is the method we reach for when the same blockage has returned, when grease coats a kitchen branch, or when roots have entered a joint.

Repeat clogs and a snake that would not clear

A cable cuts a hole through a blockage. The ring of grease, scale and sludge around that hole stays on the pipe wall and grows back, which is why a drain can flow for a month and then clog again in the same place. Jetting removes the ring itself.

Plumbers also choose it after a cable jams. A cable can wedge against a tight bend or a root mass, and forcing it risks damage to old pipe. A jet nozzle travels on a flexible hose and cleans as it moves, so it can work around the obstruction while a camera shows what is left.

Tree roots in sewer laterals

Roots find the tiny gaps at joints in clay, cast-iron or older concrete-fiber lines, then thicken into a mat that catches paper and waste. A rotating jet nozzle with a cutting head can strip the fibers back to the pipe wall, and the water flushes the debris to the main.

Roots grow back unless the joint is sealed, so jetting is often a maintenance step rather than a permanent repair. If the camera shows a cracked section or a displaced joint, we discuss sewer line repair so the entry point is closed. Some owners schedule a periodic jetting visit when the line is otherwise sound.

Emergency backup after heavy rain

The Whippany River gauge in Morristown, behind the shopping center on Cedar Knolls Avenue, read 5.11 feet on the morning of the October 30, 2017 nor'easter, up from 1.9 feet the day before. Water rising that quickly saturates the ground, and laterals with open joints can take in silt and grit along with groundwater.

After a storm like that, a line that suddenly drains slowly may carry sediment rather than grease, and jetting flushes the grit that a cable would only stir.

If water is entering the basement itself, the problem is different, and a sump pump is the first thing to check. Keep a note of when the slowdown started, since that timing helps the plumber decide whether sediment or roots are the likelier cause.

Grease, scale and kitchen branch lines

Restaurants and homes alike push cooking fat down the sink, where it cools and layers into a waxy lining. Hot water alone does not move it far. Jetting with heated water, when the equipment supports it, softens and removes the lining so the pipe returns closer to its full width.

Older cast-iron drains also build rough scale on the inside. Left alone, it narrows the pipe and catches debris, so a scoured line often runs better than it has in years. We still check the pipe condition first, since heavy pressure is not suited to every wall.

What a jetting visit involves

A jetting visit is planned around finding the blockage, clearing it, and proving the result with a camera pass when needed.

  • The plumber locates a cleanout or opens the line at a toilet or roof vent.
  • A small camera or a trial pass shows whether the pipe can take pressure.
  • The nozzle goes in on the hose and works from the access point out toward the main.
  • Water and loosened debris travel downstream, and the plumber confirms flow at the nearest fixtures.
  • You receive a plain description of what was removed and whether repairs are advised.
