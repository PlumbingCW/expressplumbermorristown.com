The main line runs from where the house drains join to the lateral that crosses your property and meets the public sewer. A plumber clears it from the cleanout, a toilet flange, or a roof vent, depending on the house. The work takes a larger cable or jetting equipment than a sink clog needs, and it ends with a check that waste is moving downstream.

Backup in the lowest drains of the house

Sewage always looks for the lowest opening, so a main line blockage shows up first at a basement shower, floor drain or laundry standpipe. These steps limit the mess while a crew is on the way.

  • Shut off washers and stop flushing, because more water only adds to the backup.
  • Find the cleanout cap, often a threaded plug in a basement wall or an outdoor pipe near the foundation.
  • Do not open the cleanout yourself unless you are ready for the pressure behind the cap.
  • Cover floor drains with a plate or mat if sewage is bubbling up, and keep pets away.
  • Bring photos and the time the problem began to the technician.

Main sewer cleaning from the cleanout

A cleanout is a capped pipe that gives direct access to the main. The plumber opens it, feeds a heavy cable down the line, and cuts through whatever has built up, usually paper, wipes, grease or a mass of roots. A final flush confirms the line drains at full speed.

If the cable keeps hitting the same obstruction or returns with fragments of pipe, the line may be damaged rather than merely clogged. In that case the visit becomes diagnosis, and the next move is a camera inspection before anyone recommends digging.

Whose sewer is it, yours or the street's

Part of Morristown's sewer system is run by the Morris Township Water Pollution Control Utility, which lists a weekday sewer line at 973-326-7363 and evening and weekend dispatch through police at 973-539-0777. If your neighbors on the street are also backing up, the blockage may be in the public main, and the utility is the right call. If only your house is affected, the clog is on your lateral and a plumber can clear it.

Either way, ask the plumber which side of the property line the blockage sat on, because that settles who handles any follow-up. For addresses that sit outside the utility's area, ask the town for the right contact.

What blocks a main line

Wipes labeled flushable are a leading cause, since they do not break down like toilet paper and snag on rough pipe walls. Grease poured into kitchen drains cools in the lateral and layers into a plug. Roots enter through joints and gather solids.

A pipe that has settled can also hold a standing pool of water, called a belly, which catches solids at the low point. Cleaning relieves the symptom, but the belly remains and may bring a call back months later. Where roots are the cause, hydro jetting often follows the first cable pass so the pipe wall is cleared.

Keeping the main clear after cleaning

A few habits cut repeat visits. Put only toilet paper in the toilet, and wipe greasy pans before washing. Keep the cleanout cap accessible, not buried under stored boxes or a shelf. If the same line clogs more than once a year, schedule a camera look and ask about periodic cleaning.
