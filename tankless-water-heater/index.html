A tankless water heater heats water only when you open a tap, so there is no storage tank to fill, hold warm or rust through. For Morristown households that want a smaller footprint in the basement and a steady supply for long showers, it can be a good fit. Our plumbers install, service and repair tankless units, with the price made clear before any work begins.

The catch is that these heaters are more sensitive to water quality and to how they are set up. Gas size, vent type and mineral scale all decide whether a tankless unit runs happily for years or becomes a nuisance.

How tankless heating works in a real house

Cold water enters the unit and passes a flow sensor. When flow reaches the minimum, a burner or electric element switches on and heats the water as it moves through a compact exchanger.

The unit can only raise temperature so far at a given flow rate. Run two showers and a dishwasher at once, and a small unit may deliver lukewarm water, so capacity has to match your busiest moment.

What an installation needs

Most tankless gas models need a larger gas line than a tank heater, since they burn fuel at a high rate. They also need sealed venting, often through a sidewall, and a condensate drain for high-efficiency models.

Electric tankless units draw heavy current and may require a dedicated circuit and panel capacity. We check these requirements first, and if your house is not ready, we will say so rather than force it. The simpler path may be a standard tank water heater installation.

Tankless water heater flush and descale

Scale is the leading cause of tankless trouble. As water heats in the exchanger, dissolved minerals drop out and coat the narrow passages, which cuts flow, raises temperatures and triggers error codes.

SMCMUA's own 2024 figures put hardness anywhere between 29 and 400 mg/L, because the Authority blends reservoir, groundwater and purchased supplies. That is a huge spread for a single utility, so test your tap before assuming your water is soft. In high readings, a yearly descale is a sensible habit, while softer addresses may stretch the interval.

How we flush a tankless unit

The job uses isolation valves on the hot and cold lines. We close the house supply, connect hoses to the service ports and circulate a descaling solution through the exchanger with a small pump, typically for a good while.

Then we flush with clean water, clear the inlet filter screen and check flow and temperature at the outlet. If your unit has no service valves, we can fit them so future maintenance takes minutes instead of an afternoon.

Signs your tankless heater needs attention

Watch for these signals before a small issue becomes a failed exchanger.

  • Error codes that return after you reset the unit.
  • Water that swings between hot and cold during a shower.
  • A rumbling or whistling sound from the cabinet.
  • Noticeably weaker hot water flow at the faucet.

Care between service visits

Clean the inlet screen when you hear flow drop, keep the area around the cabinet clear and listen for changes in sound. A damp patch under the unit is not normal, and a leak detection visit can tell a loose fitting from a hidden line.

Write the date of each descale on the wall near the unit. That small habit helps the next plumber see how fast scale returns at your address.

Repair or replace a tankless unit

Many problems are fixable. Dirty screens, failed sensors, igniters, fans and scaled exchangers can all be serviced. A cracked exchanger or repeated board failures on an older model are a different matter.

If the unit is failing and you have lost hot water entirely, our fast diagnosis and repair is available at any hour. When the repair would approach the price of a new heater, we say so and show you both options.

Water quality beyond scale

Morristown tap water is disinfected with chlorine, and the supply also carries dissolved minerals that vary by source. A whole-house filter or softener can protect the exchanger, though a softener is a household decision with salt and drain needs of its own.

If you are weighing a bigger plumbing project at the same time, such as supply upgrades, we can look at repiping and the heater together. For homes just outside town, see the townships around us.
