Water is the first thing to know about Parsippany-Troy Hills. The township covers 25.35 square miles, 1.72 of them water, and the Rockaway and Whippany rivers both run through it. Glacial Lake Passaic once covered much of the area, which is part of why so many basements here sit on soggy ground.

When a pipe bursts or a sump quits mid-storm, call (908) 357-1542. Our dispatch is staffed 24/7, and we send plumbers who can handle the plumbing side of a wet basement. Turn off the main supply, stay out of standing water near electrical equipment, and keep the line free for our callback.

Flooded basements and what Ida showed

After the September 2021 storm, Patch ran the headline that Parsippany got 6 inches of rain and major roads flooded. Homes on low ground took water through floors, window wells and floor drains, and many sump pumps were overwhelmed.

The lesson is practical. A pump with a battery backup and a clear discharge line is worth more than a new carpet. Our sump pump service covers testing, replacement and discharge checks before the next storm, and we answer after-hours calls when one fails mid-rain.

Walk your basement before the forecast turns bad. Pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch the float rise, the pump start and the water leave. If any step fails, call before the storm rather than during it.

Sewer backups when the ground is saturated

Heavy rain does not only push water in from outside. It can also load sewer lines, and a floor drain that burbles after a downpour is a warning. Sewage in a basement is a health hazard, so keep family and pets out of it.

Shut off any appliance that drains into the line and call us. We start with main sewer cleaning and add a camera look if the blockage keeps coming back. If the stoppage is out under the street, the township will need to be involved.

Lake Hiawatha, Mount Tabor and Troy Hills

Parsippany-Troy Hills is a group of communities, including Lake Hiawatha with ZIP 07034, Mount Tabor with ZIP 07878, Troy Hills and Lake Parsippany. Streets near the lakes and the rivers tend to have higher water tables. Streets on the slopes tend to have well-drained basements but longer pipe runs.

Ask yourself where your house sits before deciding what to prepare for. Low-lying homes should focus on drains and pumps. Hillside homes should look at pressure, supply valves and water heaters.

How the highways shape our routes

I-80, I-287, US 46 and the other routes through the township are fast when open and slow when closed. We route by traffic conditions on the day and tell you honestly what the road picture looks like when you call.

If a crash or a flooded road blocks our usual path, dispatch picks another. That is the benefit of a crew that knows both the highways and the back streets. Tell us the nearest cross street and we will give you the best route in plain terms.

The township groundwater system

Parsippany-Troy Hills water is run by the Township Water Department, which the township describes as a groundwater-based system. If your water suddenly turns cloudy or loses pressure on the whole street, call the township first. If only your house is affected, the problem is likely inside the property.

Hardness and taste vary, so test your tap or ask the township utility about your area. For household problems, tell us about a dial that will not stop spinning and we can start tracing it.

Denville and Mountain Lakes

Denville and Mountain Lakes sit just across the township line, and we get calls from both. Homes there share the same hilly, lake-rich ground, so many of the same basement and drain issues apply.
