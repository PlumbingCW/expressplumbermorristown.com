A sewer camera inspection shows what is happening inside a line you cannot see. Morristown Express Plumbing provides sewer inspection in Morristown, NJ, using a small waterproof camera on a flexible rod that travels from a cleanout to the public connection. Plumbers narrate what appears on screen, mark the location of any damage, and give you a clear price before work starts for anything that needs fixing.

People book this visit for two main reasons. Some are about to buy a house and want to know the condition of the line before closing. Others have a drain that keeps slowing, a lawn with a wet patch, or a past repair they want to confirm. The camera answers whether the problem is a simple clog or a pipe defect.

Inspecting a sewer line before you buy a house

A sewer line is among the most expensive hidden parts of a house to repair, and the seller's inspection report may not cover it. A camera pass before closing shows cracks, offsets, roots and bellies, and it gives you room to ask for a repair or a credit while the deal is still open. Ask your attorney or agent how long your window is, because camera visits fit best early in it.

Buyers commuting from the NJ Transit Morristown station or working near Headquarters Plaza often have little time for house hunting, so we try to offer same-day appointments when available. If the home is older, ask the camera operator to note the pipe material and any transitions between types, since a joint between cast iron and plastic is a common weak point.

What the camera looks for

The plumber watches the screen as the camera advances and notes each finding with its distance from the access point.

  • Cracks, fractures and missing pipe sections.
  • Roots entering at joints or through gaps.
  • Offsets where one length of pipe has shifted from the next.
  • Bellies, which are low spots where water and solids pool.
  • Scale, grease or debris narrowing the pipe.
  • Connections from other drains and the condition of the transition to the street connection.

Recurring clogs and slow drains

If a line has been cleared several times and keeps slowing down, the cause is usually something a cable cannot fix. The camera shows whether that is root growth, a sagging section, or a rough wall that catches material. With the footage in hand, the next step becomes obvious instead of guesswork.

Depending on what shows, we may suggest hydro jetting to clean the pipe wall, or a plan for sewer line repair if there is structural damage. A basic clog would simply call for main line cleaning.

After storms and wet seasons

The Whippany River gauge in Morristown rose from 1.9 feet to 5.11 feet within a day during the October 30, 2017 nor'easter, and the September 2021 remnants of Ida dropped roughly half a foot of rain on the area in 24 hours.

Events like these expose weak joints, because groundwater enters private laterals through any gap and carries soil with it. If your lateral slowed or backed up after a heavy rain, a camera visit afterward can show whether that was a one-time overload or a gap that will repeat.

What you receive after the visit

You get a verbal walkthrough at the cleanout with the footage on screen. We explain the pipe material, the condition, and any defects, and we describe the repair options without pushing any one. Ask for a recording or still images to keep for your records or to share with a seller, an agent or an insurer.

The camera does not remove blockages by itself, so a heavily clogged line may need cleaning before it can be inspected. The plumber will tell you if that is the case before anything extra is done.

What affects the price

The price is clear before work starts, and it depends on access and what the visit includes. A line with a ready cleanout is simpler to inspect than one reached through a toilet or roof vent.
