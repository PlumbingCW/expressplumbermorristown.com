Winter freeze-ups and burst lines

Cold snaps hit exterior walls, garages and crawl areas first. A pipe that freezes does not always burst right away. The split often opens when the ice melts, so the flood arrives after the cold has ended.

If a line is frozen but not yet leaking, open the faucet and warm the area gently with a hair dryer. Never use a flame. If it has already burst, close the main and call for burst pipe repair right away.

Insulating exposed pipe in a garage or crawl area is cheap and quick. If a line in the same wall freezes every winter, ask us about moving or rerouting it during a planned visit instead of an emergency one.

Spring thaw and basement seepage

Snowmelt and April rain push water toward foundations. Basements in Morris Plains can show damp walls or a trickle at the floor joint. A pump pit that is dry in August may fill fast in March.

Test the pit in early spring and clear the discharge path. If the pump cycles constantly or runs without moving water, our sump pump replacement and repair visits cover it. Do not wait for the heavy storm to find out.

Also look at where your gutters empty. Downspouts that dump beside the foundation add to the load the pump has to handle.

Summer drain and sewer trouble

Warm months bring heavy laundry loads, guests and grease from outdoor cooking washed down kitchen drains. Roots also grow aggressively when it is hot, and they find small gaps in old drain lines.

Most of the borough sits on the township sewer utility, so a backup that starts at the street may be the utility's problem. A backup inside your walls is ours, and drain cleaning is usually the first step.

Autumn is water heater season

Tank heaters age quietly. Sediment builds up, the anode rod wears out and a small drip turns into a failed tank right when the first cold week arrives. Rumbling noises and rust-colored hot water are early signs.

If yours is old and showing signs, plan the swap on your schedule. If it has already failed, we offer same-day water heater replacement when appointments are available.

Route 202, Route 10 and the train station

The borough sits where Route 202, Route 10 and Route 53 come together, with a stop on the NJ Transit Morristown Line. Commuters leave early and return late, so a slow leak can run all day while nobody is home.

If you travel for work, know where your main shutoff is and consider a leak sensor by the water heater. Mountain Way School and Morris Plains Borough School families should also know their shutoff.

We also serve Parsippany-Troy Hills to the north and Florham Park to the east, so a call from a commuter on the road is routed to the same dispatch desk.

Water and sewer in the borough

Water is supplied by SMCMUA, the municipal utilities authority serving the borough, and its 24-hour emergency line is 973-867-1758. Sewer for most of the borough belongs to the township's Water Pollution Control Utility.

Because SMCMUA blends sources, hardness varies from street to street and over the year. Test your tap or read the authority's water quality report before choosing any treatment equipment.
