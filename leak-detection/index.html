If you have stains, drips or a meter that will not sit still, you need leak detection in Morristown, NJ, and you need it before the damage spreads. Our dispatch runs 24/7, and licensed plumbers can often come the same day when the schedule allows.

While you wait, shut the valve nearest the wet area, or the main valve if you cannot tell where it is coming from. Move anything valuable off the floor and keep electronics clear of wet walls.

Finding a leak is detective work. Water travels along joists, pipes and framing before it shows itself, so the stain on your ceiling is rarely above the break. We listen, measure pressure, read the meter and use thermal and acoustic tools to point at the real spot, then open the smallest hole possible.

Active leak or ceiling dripping right now

Do these first while you wait for our plumber.

  • Close the shutoff valve at the fixture, or the main valve if the source is unknown.
  • Switch off power to any room with water near outlets or light fixtures.
  • Place a bucket under a drip and poke a small hole in a bulging ceiling to drain it.
  • Take photos of the damage and note the meter reading for your insurer.

How we trace a hidden leak

We begin with questions. Where did you first notice the damp spot, does it change when a fixture runs, and did it start after rain? Those answers decide whether we are chasing a pressurized supply line, a drain, or water that is not plumbing at all.

Next we isolate sections of piping and watch pressure with a gauge. Acoustic listening devices pick up the hiss of escaping water through floors and walls, and a thermal camera shows cold or warm trails where a pipe is wet. Only after the leak is located do we cut, and the hole is kept as small as the repair allows.

Water meter still running with everything off

A meter that moves when every tap, toilet and appliance is off is the clearest sign of a hidden leak. Look for the small triangle or dial that spins slowly, write down the reading, wait an hour without using water and read it again. A change means water is escaping somewhere on your side of the meter.

Toilet flappers cause many of these readings, so we check them first (a worn one is a quick toilet repair) with a dye tablet. If the toilets are clean, we test the supply pipes between the meter and the house. Southeast Morris County Municipal Utilities Authority supplies water to Morristown, and its 24-hour emergency line is 973-867-1758 if the leak looks like it is on the street side of the meter.

Ceiling and wall leaks

Brown rings on a ceiling, bubbling paint or a warm spot on a wall usually point to a supply line, a drain joint or a shower pan above. The stain tells us where water finally came out, not where it started, so we trace backward along the pipe run from the highest wet point.

Many Morristown homes are older, and those can hide galvanized supply lines that corrode from the inside and pinhole through the wall. When we find one, we repair the failed section and tell you honestly if the rest of the line is near the end, which is when repiping the house starts to make sense.

Basement, crawlspace and under-house leaks

Most houses in town sit on basements rather than slabs, so a lot of leaks reveal themselves as damp floor joists, rust on pipe hangers or a puddle that comes and goes. We look at joints, valves, the connections on the water heater and the lowest drain lines before blaming the walls.

Timing matters here. In early September 2021, the remnants of Hurricane Ida put roughly 5.5 to 6.5 inches of rain on the Morristown area in 24 hours, according to NJDEP. If your basement is wet only after storms, the water is probably coming through the foundation or floor, and a sump pump check is a better first call than a leak search.

Slab leaks, and how likely they are here

A slab leak is a leak in a pipe running under or inside a concrete floor. In this area they are less common than basement and crawlspace problems because most homes were built with basements, but additions, garages and some newer builds do have slabs.

Warning signs include a warm patch on the floor, the sound of running water when everything is off, and a high meter reading with no wet spot to explain it. If we confirm one, we explain the options, which usually come down to repairing the line in place or running a new line overhead.

Buried water line leaks

The pipe between the street connection and your house runs underground, and when it leaks you may see a soft patch of lawn, a pressure drop or a meter that will not stop. We use acoustic equipment and pipe tracing to find the line and mark where it is failing before any digging starts.

Ownership matters, because the utility is responsible for its side of the connection and you are responsible for yours. Ask your water utility where that boundary sits, and we will tell you which part of the leak we can repair.

Irrigation, pool and meter-box leaks

Sprinkler systems fail at buried valves, heads that were hit by a mower and fittings that cracked over winter. We pressure-test each zone to find the one that is losing water and mark the broken part.

Pools and spas lose water to evaporation, so we run a bucket test before suspecting the plumbing. Water pooling around the outdoor meter box is a separate case, and we check the fittings on our side before pointing you to the utility.
