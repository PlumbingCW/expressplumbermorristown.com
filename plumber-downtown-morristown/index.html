Downtown Morristown is built around the Morristown Green, with Headquarters Plaza and the NJ Transit Morristown station close by on the Morristown Line. It is the part of town where storefronts, offices and apartments sit shoulder to shoulder, and where one stopped drain can affect several tenants.

Plumbing here is different from plumbing in a detached house. Water and drain lines are often shared, access is through basements and mechanical rooms rather than yards, and a failure can interrupt a business day. This page covers the common downtown situations and what to do first.

If something is wrong now, call (908) 357-1542, give the street address and the type of space and describe what you see. Our commercial plumbing page lists what business owners usually ask about.

Shared drains and who to call first

In a multi-tenant building a backup in one unit can come from a shared drain that serves several spaces. Before calling anyone, find out whether the stoppage is only in your fixtures or also in the neighbors. If a floor drain or a lower-level toilet is backing up while upper fixtures work, the blockage is usually below you.

Tell the property manager or landlord early. Who pays for a repair often depends on your lease and on whether the problem is inside your space or in the common line, and we cannot decide that for you.

  • Note which fixtures are affected and which are fine.
  • Stop using water in the affected area.
  • Photograph standing water and take note of the time it started.
  • Tell the landlord or building manager before work begins.

Restaurants, kitchens and grease

Any kitchen that cooks with fat or oil can build up grease in its drain line. A grease trap or interceptor, where one is installed, needs regular cleaning to keep that grease out of the sewer. Whether your kitchen has one, and what the town expects for maintenance, are questions for your landlord and the town, since we do not know the setup at any specific address.

When a kitchen drain slows, hydro jetting cleans the pipe wall more thoroughly than a cable alone, and drain cleaning is often enough for a single clogged line. A camera look after cleaning shows whether grease or a damaged pipe caused it.

Water heaters and restrooms in commercial space

A dead water heater in a small office or shop does not just mean cold hand-washing. It can stop dish sanitizing, hand-wash rules and customer restrooms. Ask whether a repair or a replacement makes sense, and ask how long the work will interrupt service before you agree.

For apartments above storefronts, water heater repair often restores hot water quickly if the unit is still sound, while an old tank that leaks from the bottom is usually a replacement.

Backflow, water meters and what the town may require

Some commercial properties have backflow preventers on their water service, and these are often tested on a schedule. We do not know the requirements for a specific downtown building, so check with the town's plumbing sub-code official and your insurer. Plumbing work in New Jersey falls under the state Uniform Construction Code, which the town enforces locally, so permits and inspections for larger jobs go through that office.

Morristown's water comes from the Southeast Morris County Municipal Utilities Authority. If water pressure drops across the whole block, call the authority at 973-867-1758 before assuming your own pipes are at fault.

Old downtown buildings and new ones

Part of downtown sits inside the Morristown Historic District, which also includes the Green, the courthouse and the train station. Buildings there can be much older than the pipes inside them. Our historic district page explains how to approach an older building.

Newer downtown construction has its own issues, such as long drain stacks and shared risers. In either case, a camera inspection or a pressure test answers questions that guessing cannot.
