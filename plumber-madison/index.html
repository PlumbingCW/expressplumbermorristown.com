Madison was called Bottle Hill until 1834 and became a borough in 1889, so its housing stock spans a long stretch of building eras. The borough counts 5,989 housing units, and the plumbing in them is far from uniform. We dispatch licensed plumbers to Madison around the clock, so a Saturday night leak is handled the same as a Tuesday morning one.

If something is flooding right now, close the main valve before you pick up the phone. Then call (908) 357-1542 and tell us what the water is doing. That description helps us send the right truck, and we will tell you the price before any work starts.

Older homes near the center of town

Closer to the downtown restaurants and the historic 1916 train station, many houses date from well before copper became standard. Expect galvanized supply lines that narrow with mineral buildup and cast-iron drains that rust and flake. A slow tub and weak shower pressure on the same floor often point to this.

If that sounds familiar, a repair may only be a patch. A full repipe can be the calmer answer when several joints have already failed, and we will lay out both paths honestly.

Mid-century and newer houses

Outside the oldest blocks, copper and later PEX lines are more common. They hold up well, but the weak points move to fittings, valves and the water heater. A dripping shutoff under a sink or a stained basement ceiling under a bathroom is the usual first clue.

For a leak you cannot see, start with the meter test. Turn off everything, check whether the dial still moves, and tell us what you find. Our leak detection visits are built around that kind of clue.

Campus, offices and larger buildings

Drew University, founded in 1867, and the Giralda Farms office development are large users of plumbing. Commercial buildings add backflow devices, grease traps and big water heaters to the usual list. A failed fixture at 6 p.m. on a weeknight is still a problem for the people inside.

Landlords and building managers can reach us after hours for commercial plumbing calls too. Keep the shutoff locations posted where staff can find them.

Basements, laundry lines and slow drains

Many Madison homes have full basements, and that is where most plumbing trouble shows up first. Floor drains back up, washer hoses split, and old sink traps leak a little at a time until a shelf of stored boxes goes soft. Walk the basement once a season with a flashlight and look at pipe joints, the base of the water heater, and the floor drain.

A drain that gurgles when the washer drains usually means the line is narrowing. Catch it early and a cable clears it. Wait for the full backup and you are mopping at midnight, which we also handle, but nobody prefers it.

The borough water utility

Madison's water comes from the borough's own utility, called the Madison Water Department, rather than from a regional authority. If water looks cloudy or pressure drops across the whole street, call the borough first because the cause may be on its side of the meter.

If the trouble is only at your home, the main shutoff and the lines past the meter are your responsibility. That is where we come in. Ask the borough utility directly about your own water quality questions, since we do not want to guess.

The Chatham border

Madison borders Chatham borough to the east and Chatham Township to the south, and it is easy to lose track of which town a street belongs to. Tell us your street and house number when you call, and we will confirm the town and ZIP.

Crews cover both sides of the line, and we also work in neighboring Florham Park and Morris Township. A backed-up kitchen line on a border street still gets drain cleaning the same day when appointments are open.
