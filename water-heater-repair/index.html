Most water heater repair in Morristown starts with a simple question. Is the water coming from a fitting, a valve or the tank shell? That answer decides whether a repair makes sense, so we check it before anything is taken apart.

Water heater leaking right now

A steady drip changes what you do in the first ten minutes. Use these steps before the plumber gets there.

  • Shut off the gas control knob or the electric breaker so the heater stops firing.
  • Close the cold water supply valve above the tank to stop it refilling.
  • Move stored items and electronics off the floor around the heater.
  • Check whether the drip is at a pipe joint, the relief valve or the bottom of the tank.

Where the water is coming from decides the repair

Water at a pipe connection on top of the tank is often a loose union or a worn supply line. Those are quick fixes. Water from the temperature and pressure relief valve points to excess pressure or a valve that has gone bad, and the cause has to be found before a new valve is fitted.

Water pooling under the tank is different. When the steel shell has rusted through from the inside, no patch will hold, and we will tell you so plainly. A tank in that state needs the replacement route described on our tank replacement page.

No hot water from a gas, electric or hybrid tank

Each type fails in its own way. A gas heater that will not stay lit usually has a dirty burner, a weak thermocouple or a failed gas control valve. An electric heater with lukewarm water often has a burned-out heating element or a tripped high-limit switch.

Hybrid heat pump units add a compressor, a fan and a condensate drain to the list of parts that can fail. They also have a resistance element that keeps the tank hot in backup mode, so a unit that seems fine but runs slowly can be hiding a heat pump fault. Our plumbers carry common parts and test the full system, not just the part that looks guilty.

Repairs we make most often

Thermostats, upper and lower elements, thermocouples, pilot assemblies, relief valves, drain valves and dip tubes make up most of the parts we replace. A worn anode rod is another frequent finding, because that rod sacrifices itself to protect the tank steel and stops working once it is eaten away.

Replacing a spent anode early can add useful life to a tank that is otherwise sound. We will show you the old part so the diagnosis is not a matter of trust alone.

Sediment, hard water and what to do about it

Hardness in the Southeast Morris County Municipal Utilities Authority system ranged from 29 to 400 mg/L in 2024, because the utility blends reservoir water with several groundwater sources. Mineral build-up inside a tank can therefore differ from street to street, so test your tap. If your water runs hard, a tank that rumbles when it heats needs a flush before sediment bakes onto the bottom.

When repair is the wrong call

A heater with a failed thermostat is worth fixing. A heater that is leaking from the shell, rusting at the base or repeatedly tripping its safety devices is a different story, and paying for parts on a worn-out tank rarely pays back.

We will lay out both options with a clear price before work starts. If the unit is sound and the part is cheap, we repair it. If a new heater is the better value, you will hear that too, and you can read how installs are handled on the planned installation page.
