Most toilet calls come down to a handful of parts. A worn flapper, a tired fill valve, a failed wax seal or a cracked bowl accounts for nearly every leak and every phantom refill. We diagnose first, explain what failed, and put a clear price on the table first, so you can choose between a repair and a new toilet with the facts in hand.

Toilet overflowing or leaking onto the floor

Overflow has two common causes. Either the bowl is clogged and the water has nowhere to go, or the drain behind it is partly blocked and the clog is bigger than the toilet. The stop valve buys you time in both cases, because it ends the supply even when the bowl keeps draining slowly.

  • Close the stop valve behind the toilet by turning it clockwise
  • Do not flush again, even if the water level drops
  • Move rugs and towels away from the wet area
  • Check the ceiling below if the bathroom is on an upper floor
  • Tell us if other drains gurgle, since that points to the main line

Running toilets and phantom refills

A toilet that hisses, refills on its own or runs for minutes after a flush is losing water into the bowl. The usual culprit is a flapper that no longer seats, a chain with too much slack, or a fill valve that cannot shut off cleanly. These are small parts, but a constant trickle adds up on the water bill over a month.

Sinks and showers in the same bathroom often wear out on a similar schedule, which is where faucet and valve repair fits in. Try a drop of food coloring in the tank and wait a quarter hour without flushing. If color shows up in the bowl, the flapper is leaking and we can usually replace it on the first visit.

Leaks at the base, the tank bolts and the supply line

Water around the base usually means the wax seal under the toilet has failed, or the bowl is rocking and has broken it. Water under the tank points to worn tank bolts or the gasket between tank and bowl. Water at the wall points to the braided supply line or the stop valve itself.

Finding the exact source matters because mopping the floor only hides it. A slow base leak can soak the subfloor for months before anyone notices. In a Morristown house with a finished basement or a room below the bathroom, the first sign is often a stained ceiling, so check there as well. If a supply line has split and the water will not stop, treat it as a burst pipe repair call.

Toilet repair or a new toilet installation

A toilet in good condition with one worn part deserves a repair. A bowl with hairline cracks, a tank that sweats badly, or a flush that clogs weekly is a better candidate for replacement. We set the new toilet on a fresh wax seal, check that the floor flange is sound, and level the bowl so it does not rock.

Houses here range from colonial-era stock, since Morristown was settled in 1715, to mid-century builds. Older bathrooms can hide a rotted flange or a cast-iron drain stub under the toilet. We check both before the new bowl goes down, because a bad flange is far cheaper to fix with the toilet already off.

When the clog is bigger than the toilet

If plunging works but the problem returns within days, the blockage is probably past the trap. We bring a closet auger for the bowl and, when needed, run a cable down the line through our drain cleaning service. If several fixtures back up together, the trouble is likely the main drain and not the toilet.

Morristown sewers are run by different authorities depending on the street. The Morris Township Water Pollution Control Utility serves parts of Morristown and takes calls on its weekday sewer line at 973-326-7363. If the blockage is out at the street, that call tells you whose pipe it is. If it is on your side, main sewer line cleaning clears it.

Mineral buildup and weak flushes

Tap water in this area is blended from several sources, and the Southeast Morris County Municipal Utilities Authority reported hardness from 29 to 400 mg/L in its 2024 water quality report. That is a wide swing. On the harder end, scale narrows the rim jets under the bowl lip and clogs the small siphon jet, so the flush turns weak and sluggish.

Test your tap or read the utility report to see where your street falls. If scale is the cause, cleaning the jets can restore the flush without buying a new bowl.
