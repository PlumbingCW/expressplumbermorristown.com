Plumbing installation in Morristown, NJ covers more than hanging a new faucet. It includes moving a sink during a kitchen remodel, adding a bathroom in a finished basement, running lines for a laundry room and setting up fixtures in a new addition. Licensed plumbers handle the pipe, the connections and the testing, and you receive a clear price before work starts.

Most installation jobs are planned, so the best time to call is before the cabinets are ordered or the drywall goes up. A short walkthrough early on saves a lot of rework later, because the position of a drain decides where a toilet or shower can go.

Gas smell safety note

If you smell gas, do not turn lights on or off, do not use your phone inside and do not call us first. Leave the building right away, then call your gas utility or 911 from outside. Once the area is declared safe, we can help with any plumbing repairs that follow.

Fixtures and appliances we install

We install toilets, sinks, faucets, showers, tubs, dishwashers, washing machine hookups, outdoor spigots and utility sinks. We supply the connections and supply lines, and you can bring your own fixtures if you already chose them.

New fixtures need the right valve, trap and drain configuration for the house. Where a fixture fails after installation, our faucet repair and toilet repair teams can handle it.

Remodels and moving plumbing

Kitchen and bathroom remodels often shift fixtures a few feet. Moving a sink is straightforward, but moving a toilet or tub can mean cutting into a floor and re-routing a drain at the right slope.

Many Morristown houses have basements, which helps because pipes are visible overhead and a drain can often be reached from below. In finished or plaster-walled houses we plan access holes in advance so you know what gets opened and what gets patched.

New construction and additions

For an addition or new build we work from your plans in two stages. Rough-in puts the supply and drain pipes in before walls close, and finish plumbing sets the fixtures once surfaces are done.

Ask your builder and the town construction office which permits and inspections apply to your project. We coordinate the schedule with the rest of your trades so the rough-in is ready on the date the framing is.

Water heaters and appliance hookups

A new water heater is a plumbing installation as well, and it needs correct venting, shutoffs and an expansion tank where appropriate. We cover the details on our water heater installation page.

Washing machines and dishwashers need their own supply valves and drain connections, and the wrong hose or an undersized drain causes leaks later. We install the proper valves and test each connection under load.

Old pipe in new projects

Opening a wall for a remodel often exposes the pipe that has been inside for decades. In older houses that may be galvanized steel or cast iron, and tying new fixtures into worn pipe can bring pressure or drain problems.

If we find tired pipe, we show you and explain the choice between connecting to it and replacing that run. For larger replacements, repiping can be done while the walls are already open.

Planning before you buy fixtures

Check the rough-in dimensions before you order a toilet, vanity or tub, because a few inches decide whether it fits. Ask us to confirm the drain location and supply valve position, and send photos if you cannot meet on site.

Tell us what the water in your tap is like too. Water that varies from street to street, as SMCMUA's blended sources do, can leave scale on fixtures, so ask your utility or test your tap before choosing finishes and valves.

How an installation visit goes

We look at the space, confirm the fixture list and explain what the job involves. After you approve the price, we protect your floors, shut off water, install and test the work.

We leave the area clean and walk you through shutoff valve locations. Same-day appointments are possible when available, though installations are best scheduled ahead.
