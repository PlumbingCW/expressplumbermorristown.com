Speedwell Avenue runs through the heart of Morristown and is named for the Speedwell Ironworks, where the electric telegraph was first shown to the public on January 11, 1838. Today the corridor mixes older houses with newer rental buildings, and the Whippany River winds through the same part of town.

That mix shapes what goes wrong. Apartment dwellers deal with shared stacks and shutoffs, while homes and buildings near low ground deal with basement water. This page covers both, and says clearly where we have facts and where we do not.

If a pipe, drain or basement is giving you trouble, call the Morristown line at (908) 357-1542 and describe what is happening.

Apartments and the Speedwell redevelopment

The Speedwell Redevelopment is a four-phase plan approved in 2012 for the avenue. Its first phase, called Latitude, was reported by Patch in January 2013 as 268 rental units, including 26 deed-restricted affordable units, with a 357-space parking garage.

Large rental buildings plumb differently from houses. Water usually comes in through a main, runs up shared risers and drains down shared stacks. If you rent, the first step for a leak or backup is to tell the building manager, because shutting off the wrong valve or opening the wrong wall can affect other units.

  • Know where your unit's shutoff valve is and test that it turns.
  • Report slow drains early before a shared line backs up.
  • Report a ceiling stain to management in writing with photos.
  • Do not run a drain cleaner product into a shared line.

Older houses near the avenue

Away from the newer buildings are older houses with basements. We do not know the age or pipe material of any specific house, and we do not assume a problem from age alone. A sewer camera inspection answers it for your line.

If you notice slow drains in several fixtures, gurgling or a damp basement floor drain, the main line is the place to look. A drain cleaning can clear a stoppage, and a camera shows whether a root, a belly in the pipe or a break caused it.

The Whippany River and basement water

The Whippany River runs through Morristown. A National Weather Service gauge behind the shopping center on Cedar Knolls Avenue read 5.11 feet on the morning of October 30, 2017, up from 1.9 feet the day before, with minor flooding beginning at 6 feet. In August 2011, Tropical Storm Irene flooded parts of Morristown's Second Ward, including Martin Luther King Avenue, according to Morristown Green.

The remnants of Hurricane Ida on September 1 and 2, 2021 brought about 5.5 to 6.5 inches of rain to the Morristown area within 24 hours, according to NJDEP figures reported by Patch. We are not saying any specific Speedwell Avenue address floods. If you want to know about your lot, check the FEMA flood map and ask the town.

Sump pumps and keeping a basement dry

In a basement that takes on groundwater during storms, a working sump pump is the first defense. Test it before storm season by pouring water into the pit and checking that it switches on and pumps out. A pump that runs constantly, rattles or cycles on and off may be failing.

If water enters through walls or floors in more than one place, basement waterproofing is a larger conversation, with honest trade-offs between interior drains and exterior work. Get a clear explanation and price before agreeing to either.

Water, sewer and who to call

Morristown's water comes from the Southeast Morris County Municipal Utilities Authority, and its emergency line is 973-867-1758. The town handles sewer service, though parts of town near the boundary may connect through Morris Township, so ask the town which system serves your street.

For anything inside your walls, floor drains, fixtures or water heater, a plumber is the right call. For a main street break or a whole-block outage, call the utility.
